Can you fact-check a robot?

A computer program looked at photos and made claims like “the book is on the box”. Some are right, some are wrong. You judge them.

🎯

Each photo shows one thing in a red box and another in a blue box, plus a claim about them.

⚡

Tap TRUE only if the claim is clearly true. Wrong or can’t tell? Tap WRONG. Be strict!

📷

Left / right / in front / behind are always from the camera’s point of view: in front of = closer to the camera, behind = further away. On / under means physically resting on — a held object doesn’t count.

⏱️ Takes about 3 minutes · 15 photos

About this study

This quiz collects anonymous judgments for an MSc Data Science dissertation (University of Surrey) that evaluates an automatic image-annotation tool. No names, emails, or IP addresses are collected — only your answers. Taking part is voluntary and you can stop at any time by closing the page; by playing you agree your anonymous answers may be used in the dissertation. The collection is covered by a University of Surrey ethics self-assessment. Photos are from A Spatial Relationship Aware Dataset for Robotics (Wang et al., 2025), CC-BY 4.0; faces are blurred. The rules

  • TRUE = the claim is clearly true in the photo.
  • WRONG = the claim is wrong or you can’t tell. When in doubt, WRONG.
  • on / under: the thing physically rests on the other (or the reverse). A held object does not count as resting.
  • left / right / in front of / behind: from the camera’s point of view. “A in front of B” = A is closer to the camera, B is further away. “A behind B” = A is further from the camera than B.
  • near: the two things are next to each other, close relative to their size.
  • The red box is the first thing in the claim, the blue box the second. Judge those exact ones, even if similar objects are nearby.
